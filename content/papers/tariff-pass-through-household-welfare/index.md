##### Abstract

How far do tariff reductions reach consumers, and how does pass-through shape their distributional effects? Peru's 2007–2011 tariff cuts were largely unilateral. We combine reconstructed applied duties, monthly border and retail prices for eight foods, input-output accounts and household data to trace tariff changes into household budgets. Retail pass-through of border price movements averages 0.34, near one for sugar and oils and lower for processed staples, and the estimates for several staples fall further with month effects. Under the stated pass-through and incidence assumptions, the change from the 2007 average tariff to 2012 implies household welfare gains of 0.36 to 0.51 percent of spending. At the reference calibration, gains are modestly larger for poorer households, and their ranking depends on non-food pass-through and on the measurement of price-band duties. Engel-curve estimates imply small reductions in calorie deficits through real income. Retail price transmission shapes the distribution of the gains from trade reform.
