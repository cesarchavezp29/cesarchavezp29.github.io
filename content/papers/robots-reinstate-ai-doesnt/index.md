##### Abstract

Robot-exposed occupations create one and a half to two times as much
complementary work as AI-exposed occupations over the robot wave, and less
afterwards. Whether that reflects a property of the two technologies or the
distance each has travelled is what this paper decomposes. A two-frontier task
model writes the reinstated mass as a reinstatement rate times how far the
frontier has advanced, so in logs the gap splits into a frontier-width term and
a rate term, which a task panel over two decades of occupational data measures.
Frontier width carries most or all of it: across nine measures the implied
relative rate straddles one, against the near five a calibration of this model
imports. The reinstatement gap is mostly an exposure gap, on the reading that
automation thresholds stand for frontier levels and a task description for one
unit of the model's continuum.
