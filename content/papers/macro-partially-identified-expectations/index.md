##### Abstract

Can a macroeconomic counterfactual be identified if we know the distribution of expectations mechanisms but not which households hold them? This paper shows that it need not be. Under a scalar responsiveness channel and a compatible resource condition, the unknown assignment enters equilibrium through one statistic, responsiveness among unconstrained households. Rearrangement gives sharp bounds on this statistic, while endogenous household composition makes its admissible values an equilibrium object. In a benchmark economy, the identified set for the aggregate spans 25.93 percent of its midpoint. Without a restriction linking observed groups to the economic state, average responsiveness by income or liquidity group does not narrow the set, even with arbitrarily large samples. A classification that refines the state partition can identify the statistic from group shares and group averages using repeated cross-sections. In the benchmark, proportional income incidence makes the observable spending contrast zero under every admissible assignment.
