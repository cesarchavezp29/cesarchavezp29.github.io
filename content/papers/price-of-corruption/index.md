##### Abstract

This paper measures what corruption costs when the public works it affected can be identified. I combine two Peruvian legal records, the documented bribes in Odebrecht infrastructure projects and an antitrust decision that identifies bid rigging in road procurement. Among twenty-five Odebrecht projects, mean cost overruns were 53 percent for those with documented bribes and 17 percent for the others. In colluded road tenders, the median winning bid reached 108 percent of the government's reference value, compared with 90 percent in competitive tenders, and the difference remains 8.2 percentage points within winning economic groups and years. Households near colluded roads had income and consumption approximately 13 percent lower and poverty 6.0 percentage points higher than households near matched competitively procured roads, with the largest differences close to the works. The adjudicated tenders also allow me to evaluate collusion screens, and winning-bid levels distinguish colluded from competitive tenders with an area under the ROC curve of 0.86, compared with 0.50 for bid dispersion. These results show how procurement collusion suppresses competitive discounts and how its economic consequences may extend beyond contract prices.

##### What the paper does

**Prices the distortion.** Peruvian road tenders admit bids only inside a band around a published reference
value, so the cartel did not need to bid high, only to stop discounting. Colluded winners sit at a median of
108 percent of the reference value, while competitive winners from the same firms sit at a median of 90
percent, close to the floor of the band. The raw gap is 11.5 points, it falls to 9.5 with year, terrain,
works-type and size controls and to 8.2 once the number of bidders enters, and holding the winning economic
group and the year fixed it is still 8.2. That last estimate is identified by the fourteen groups that won
tenders of both kinds, so the gap belongs to the tender and not to which firms bid.

**Validates the detection screens against a verdict.** A level screen on the winning bid reaches an area under
the ROC curve of 0.86 against the tender-by-tender classification of the competition authority, while the
dispersion screen most often used sits at 0.50, no better than chance, and the number of bidders at 0.55.
Flagging a tender when the winning bid exceeds the reference value classifies 86 percent of tenders correctly,
and training the screen on tenders awarded up to 2009 and testing it on later ones, or the reverse, gives an
area under the curve between 0.80 and 0.94.

**Locates who bears the cost.** Two to four years after the tender, households near a colluded road have
income and consumption about 14 log points lower and poverty 6.0 points higher than households near a
comparable competitively procured road, which I pair with it on geography, award year, reference value and
terrain. The gap is largest within five kilometres of the routes, and widening both catchments to forty
kilometres lowers the income gap from 0.137 to 0.080 log points. Inside the affected radius the loss is in
earnings, mostly in what the self-employed earn, while employment rates and hours change little.
