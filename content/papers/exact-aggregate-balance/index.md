##### Abstract

Weighting estimators for clustered data often impose exact aggregate balance as a hard constraint and control within-cluster balance by penalty. The constraint does not make imbalance disappear. It removes the pooled residual by changing the imbalance left inside the clusters. At a fixed support it splits one required correction across the clusters, and relative donor support decides each cluster's share. The induced change in the heterogeneity component of bias equals the alignment of that movement with prognostic heterogeneity, so no ordering of that component holds uniformly across outcome models. The constraint is neutral exactly when it moves every cluster alike. The dispersion of the movement across clusters is the exact worst case of the change, and it is available at the design stage. On a replicated education design, the constraint moves the weighted contrast by about two thirds of the published estimate.
