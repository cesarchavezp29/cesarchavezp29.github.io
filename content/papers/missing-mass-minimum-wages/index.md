##### Abstract


How does the wage distribution change when a national minimum wage rises? Peru raised its floor four times between 2016 and 2025, by a cumulative 51 per cent. With no untreated region, I compare each post-increase distribution with counterfactuals whose forecast errors I measure in years without an increase. After every increase the share of dependent workers just below the new floor falls and the share just above it rises. In the quarterly survey and the three earlier increases, the rise concentrates in the first S/25 above the floor, more than at any other round wage. For the 2025 increase, observed in two national surveys, the best-validated counterfactuals give a fall of 5 to 7 percentage points below the floor and a rise of 4 to 6 in the first S/250 above it. Quarterly data show it starting in the first quarter under the new floor, mostly within three quarters.
