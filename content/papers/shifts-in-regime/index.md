##### Abstract


We ask whether coming of age during a transition to democracy durably shapes political attitudes. The design compares birth cohorts within the same country and survey wave, using the World Values Survey in fifty countries and Latinobarómetro in fourteen Latin American countries, all of which democratized after 1980. Formative exposure to post-transition years shows no statistically detectable positive association with regime support in any of six estimates. The most precise exclude associations above 0.01 standard deviations per year but not the smaller effects reported for lifetime exposure, and estimates by age window give no evidence that the formative years weigh more. Democratic experience is not irrelevant. Two continuous indices of the level of democracy experienced in youth are positively associated with preferring democracy to other regimes, and both associations survive a correction for multiple testing. Tolerance rises steeply across cohorts. The observed cohort patterns are not fully explained by transition-dated formative exposure.
