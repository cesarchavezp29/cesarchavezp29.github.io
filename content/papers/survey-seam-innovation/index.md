##### Abstract

Linked innovation surveys are the main evidence on R&D persistence. In Colombian and Ecuadorian surveys, firms report two or three annual R&D amounts per questionnaire. Among the same firms, 5 to 18 percent of R&D performers stop between two years of one questionnaire, and 52 to 70 percent between the last year of one questionnaire and the first of the next. Machinery spending shows the same jump. Because boundaries fall in different years in the two countries, the jump follows the questionnaire and not the calendar. For the same Ecuadorian firms, survey reports of employment and sales diverge from tax and social security records more often across the boundary than within a questionnaire, while the records show no break. Annual R&D persistence is three to four times higher within questionnaires than across them. A measurement model reconciles part of this pattern but does not identify true persistence.
