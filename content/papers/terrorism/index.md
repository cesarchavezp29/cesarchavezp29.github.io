##### Abstract

How do regions recover from civil conflict? This paper follows Peru's 1981 census districts through 1993, 2007, and 2017 to study the 1980–2000 internal armed conflict, which claimed about 69,000 lives. In the highlands, the most affected districts lost about 21 percent of their population relative to other highland districts by 1993, a loss concentrated in Ayacucho, and they had recovered about half of that loss by 2007. The cohort of young adults at the war's onset and the small districts where violence reached a large share of residents did not recover. The literacy gap with other districts fell 61 percent between 1981 and 2017. Infrastructure recovered unevenly, as services delivered through public networks lagged while services built on site expanded faster. Night-time lights show that light covered less of the territory of the most affected districts. Recovery was demographically uneven, educationally convergent, and spatially concentrated.
