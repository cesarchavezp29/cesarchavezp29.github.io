##### Abstract


How do regions recover from civil conflict? To answer this question, this paper uses Peruvian census data from 1981, 1993, 2007, and 2017 to study the 1980–2000 internal armed conflict. The paper documents three major findings related to demographic composition, human capital, and infrastructure. First, in the highlands, the most affected districts lost about 21 percent of their population relative to other highland districts by 1993, a loss concentrated mainly in Ayacucho. By 2007, the estimated deficit had narrowed to 12 percent. Second, the cohort aged 15–29 in 1981 remained underrepresented, and small districts where violence reached a large share of residents had not recovered their population by 2017. The literacy gap with other districts fell 61 percent between 1981 and 2017. Finally, infrastructure recovered unevenly, as services delivered through public networks lagged while services built on site expanded faster. Night-time lights show a widening gap in the share of district territory covered by light. Recovery was uneven across places and cohorts, as well as across dimensions of human capital and infrastructure.
