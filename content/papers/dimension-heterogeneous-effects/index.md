##### Abstract

Empirical models routinely let coefficients vary with observed characteristics, turning heterogeneity into a matrix of interaction coefficients. We define its dimension as the smallest rank reproducing that heterogeneity up to a declared prediction-loss tolerance. The loss uses the metric the design induces, so the answer does not depend on covariate scaling. Estimating the rank is a geometric problem. The plug-in loss is off-centre at second order, and the projection behind it can be non-unique. Inference therefore takes two forms, one regularity-free and one sharper but local. In the National Job Corps Study, ninety-six coefficients describe how training affects earnings by background. They compress to two channels once the tolerated error reaches $2.03 a week, against $6.19 for discarding the heterogeneity. Under a declared regularity class, inference places the dimension at two or below at the three tolerances from $2.50 up, without separating two from the lower ranks.
