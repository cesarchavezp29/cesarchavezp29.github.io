##### Abstract

How does electoral pressure change the factual content of what incumbents say in public? We study campaign tweets of U.S. House incumbents in the 2018 to 2022 partisan primaries and measure the threat with challenger fundraising public sixteen weeks before the vote. In the final four weeks, threatened incumbents post 0.77 more campaign tweets per day than other incumbents, nearly doubling their activity, and the gap holds against incumbents who also faced a ballot opponent. Two language models coded a probability sample of 600 tweets. Threatened incumbents make 0.65 more checkable claims per day, four fifths of it through higher volume, and most additional claims are electoral information such as election dates and endorsements. A two-phase documentary verification finds strict contradictions infrequent among resolved claims and no detectable rise in the share of problematic claims among threatened incumbents. Electoral pressure expands factual communication, mostly about the election itself.
