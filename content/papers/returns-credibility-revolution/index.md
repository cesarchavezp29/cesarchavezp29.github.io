##### Abstract

Credibility revolution methods reshaped empirical social science. This paper asks what they return in the market for publications. I follow 13,613 NBER working papers from posting to publication, correct their publication record with an AI-assisted audit and imputation, and add 26,745 published articles across four disciplines, reading each paper's design from its abstract. Design-based papers differ mainly in where and in what form they publish, and much less in whether they publish at all. Regression discontinuity papers appear in twenty leading journals 21.5 percentage points more often than comparable papers, against a base rate of 35 percent, and 19.7 points less often elsewhere, so publication anywhere moves by 1.8 points, and by 10.1 counting journal articles alone. Arrival rates by destination repeat the pattern. Citations favour these papers from two years after publication, unevenly across cohorts.
