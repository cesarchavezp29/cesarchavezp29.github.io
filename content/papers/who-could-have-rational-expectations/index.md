##### Abstract

Tests of rational expectations ask whether the hypothesis can be rejected. This paper asks how much of a population could still be rationalized as satisfying it. Writing beliefs and realizations as a mixture of a rational component and an unrestricted alternative, the mixing weight is bounded above but never below. The upper bound has a closed form for binary outcomes and falls as the analyst attributes more information to the agent. In the Health and Retirement Study, full rational expectations is rejected decisively, while the non-rationalizable share is small.
