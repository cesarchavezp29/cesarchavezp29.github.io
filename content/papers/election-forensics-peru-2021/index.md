##### Abstract


Peru's 2021 presidential runoff was decided by 44,263 votes. The losing campaign then filed 942 nullity petitions against 768 polling stations, and the courts dismissed almost all of them on procedure. This paper links every mesa-level petition to the official returns to study how the campaign chose its targets and which parts of its allegations the published returns can evaluate. The petitions went overwhelmingly to districts the opponent won and, within them, to unanimous mesas, capturing more net votes than any of 10,000 random draws matched on department or first-round share, and more than all but one of 10,000 draws matched on district, a pattern consistent with targeting by electoral stakes. On turnout and the joint extreme-turnout, extreme-share corner, the challenged mesas show none of the anomalies examined, a result that is informative only under stated comparability assumptions. Vote switching inside the challenged mesas cannot be separated from the selection with these data, and last-digit tests detected almost none of the simulated count-consistent manipulations at Peruvian mesa sizes.
