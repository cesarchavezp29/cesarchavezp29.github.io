##### Abstract

States require coercive organisations strong enough to extract from society yet controllable enough for the ruler to survive. These opposing requirements act on a single object, the apparatus itself, because the coordination that makes a force able to repress is the coordination that makes it able to depose, and together they bound sustainable extraction. Under a general coupling between repression and internal challenge, resources cannot relax that bound: where abundance scales the gains from extraction and the costs of conflict proportionally, additional resources leave the boundary where it was. Monitoring enlarges the coalition it polices, since the apparatus built to watch the apparatus is itself composed of claimants. When the bound falls to what society would concede without coercion, no standing extractive state can be sustained at any level of wealth.
